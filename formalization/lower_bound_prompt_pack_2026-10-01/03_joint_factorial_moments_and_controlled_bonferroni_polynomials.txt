ASSIGNED MATHEMATICS AND IMPLEMENTATION
Prove the two finite ingredients for occupancy approximation, independently of the thinning implementation.

1. For distinct complete words w_i and orders r_i with R=sum r_i, expand the product of falling factorial occupancies into ordered DISJOINT labelled-row requests. Prove the exact formula (N)_R product_j[product_a(n_{j,a})_(m_{j,a})/(N)_R]. Explain disjointness using distinctness of full words.
2. Under selected piles ≥cN, K≤C log N and R≤N^(1/5), obtain an explicit uniform relative error bounded by C1(K+1)R²/N for large N. Derive it from controlled log falling-factorial ratios, with quantitative positivity and denominators. Treat R=0 and K=0. First/second moment results already exist; they do not supply this arbitrary mixed moment theorem.
3. Prove residual factorial versions for an admissible positive-probability prescription of b labelled rows, with b≤b0<N and fixed b0. Derive the residual pile lower bound and the residual mean ratio 1+O((K+1)b/N), in finite quantified form with constants and thresholds allowed to depend on b0,c,C. Connect all moments to the canonical matrix law, not an abstract family merely asserted to have those moments.
4. For even m≥2 define U_m(n)=sum_{r=2}^m (-1)^r(r-1) choose(n,r) and D_m(n)=(m-1)choose(n,m). Prove 0≤U_m(n)-1_{n≥2}≤D_m(n) for every natural n, including n=0,1 and m=2.
5. Prove the product remainder bound by D_m(X)+D_m(Y)+D_m(X)D_m(Y). Under mixed factorial errors through TOTAL order 2m, prove the finite comparison in lem:central-window with f(t)=1-(1+t)e^(-t) and d_m(t)=(m-1)t^m/m!. Include the one-variable version and all explicit constants.
6. Prove the rare-category sandwich (1/2)E(X)_2-(1/3)E(X)_3 ≤ P(X≥2) ≤ (1/2)E(X)_2. The third coefficient is 1/3, not the old false 1/6.

The key audit is the polynomial remainder on large-count outcomes. A small probability of {X+Y>R} does NOT control the polynomial expectation there. The finite sandwich must bound it using factorial moments. No Poisson or independence approximation may be assumed as an additional hypothesis of the concrete moment theorem.
