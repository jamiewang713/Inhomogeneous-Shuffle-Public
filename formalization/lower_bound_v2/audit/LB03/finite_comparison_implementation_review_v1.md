- `Basic.expect_const` proves normalization from the actual PMF finite sum, and expectation monotonicity uses nonnegative real PMF weights. The uniform product expectation is derived from the actual uniform finite-product law. There is no independence premise attached to the occupancy statistics. `choose_eq_falling_div` uses the exact factorial/binomial identity. `rare_pointwise` handles 0, 1, 2 and the remaining natural counts, and `rareSandwich` exports exactly the corrected third-order coefficient `1/3`.
- `PolynomialMoments.factorialCoefficient` divides by `r!`, so its expectation expansion is in falling factorials rather than binomial coefficients. Absolute coefficient sums retain `(r-1)`, and the double sum uses the actual mixed moment of total order `r+s`. `expect_remainder_product_le` uses the `(m,m)` moment directly. It does not bound the product of two random remainders by separate marginal expectations.
- `Bonferroni.bonferroni_closed` is an exact formula with nonnegative binomial coefficients, avoiding a restriction on large outcomes. The implementation handles `n<2` separately, obtains both even and adjacent odd truncations, and includes `m=2`. `product_remainder` uses only scalar indicators/benchmarks in `[0,1]` and the two pointwise remainder inequalities. Its cross term is retained.
- `Central.probability_polynomial_error` integrates the entire pointwise sandwich. The two-variable proof explicitly extracts marginal moments by setting the unused order to zero and invokes the `(m,m)` moment for the product remainder. The actual remainder is bounded by `(1+ε)T`, the scalar benchmark remainder by `T`, and the coefficient error separately. The final coefficient `3` follows from `ε≤1`, with exactly the frozen `ε(1+μ)(1+ν)exp(μ+ν)` term. The one-variable conclusion has the exact frozen constants as well.
- `PoissonPolynomial` constructs summable scalar weights and derives their binomial moments by shifting the exponential series. It does not instantiate a theorem requiring a finite sample space on the natural-number Poisson law. The factorial denominator cancellations are restricted to nonzero natural factorials. The collision benchmark equals the full weight sum after removing the `n=0,1` terms. The finite Bonferroni inequalities are then summed over all natural counts, so there is no uncontrolled polynomial tail. The coefficient sum is bounded by `t exp(t)`, then by the requested weaker `(1+t)exp(t)` for `t≥0`.
