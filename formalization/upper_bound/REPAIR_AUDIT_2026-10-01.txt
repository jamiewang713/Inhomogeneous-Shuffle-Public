RESULT AND SCOPE

The manuscript now supplies a complete mathematical argument for the empirical upper bound, followed by the buffered profile upper bound and a qualified critical-time consequence. It replaces the former ambient first-moment and conditional-preservation route by direct empirical estimates and exact conditional sampling. This is a manuscript proof repair, not a new Lean formalization of the upper bound.

The empirical theorem, thm:ub-empirical-upper (source line 2383), assumes a fixed negative empirical margin and the stated local-good-column condition. It gives a uniform polynomial total-variation bound for all admissible deterministic cut arrays. It uses no macroscopic time profile.

The buffered profile theorem, thm:deterministic-upper-bound (line 371), transfers bounded scalar costs from a sufficiently fine fixed partition and sufficiently small fixed time mesh, in the order of quantifiers stated there. The partition accuracy is chosen before the good-column constants; the time mesh is chosen afterward. It uses the existing ordinary-integral convention.

The critical-time theorem, thm:upper-critical-reduced (line 344), explicitly uses def:ub-arbitrarily-fine-approximation (line 322). This requires suitable arbitrarily fine profile partitions and approximation meshes, with the good-column constants fixed before the time mesh is decreased. This is stronger than a hypothesis asserting approximation on only one arbitrary fixed coarse partition or mesh. The present proof does not recover a critical-time claim from that weaker hypothesis. The empty negativity set has critical time +infinity and yields no finite upper bound.

PROOF-OBLIGATION LEDGER

1. Graph mixture and truncation.
   The exact component factorial ratio is bounded by a fixed factor per shared edge when one graph has uniformly bounded components. The truncated chi-square radicand is handled as an integrated variance; the integrand is not falsely assumed pointwise nonnegative. The largest-component tail follows from the exact fixed-population falling-factorial bound.
   Source: lem:ub-component-factorial, lem:ub-truncated-chi-square, lem:ub-largest-component-tail.

2. Empirical first moment.
   An exposed first-column pile leaves an outside population reserve. The proof tracks the original log N scale even for smaller induced decks. Anchored endpoint smoothing, relevant-parent localization, stable stopping and explicit cost bins control the shared-edge first moment directly. Full-word terminal cases and omitted nonrelevant blocks are estimated separately. The direct large-block bound avoids an unnecessary squared endpoint-window loss.
   Source: prop:ub-first-moment (line 666), lem:ub-anchor-endpoint-atoms (line 938), lem:ub-edge-density-bounds (line 1339).

3. Stability under empirical perturbations.
   An explicit scalar margin inequality replaces the unsupported assertion that an induced probability vector stays in its old partition cell. Both perturbation parameters are nonnegative, and the margin losses and prefix-deletion cost are displayed.
   Source: lem:ub-conditional-preservation (line 1598).

4. Conditional exploration law.
   Lexicographic histories constrain only words in the selected initial pile. Exact falling-factorial sampling weights and a fixed outside reserve bound continuation counts, current-run extras and suffix collisions. Auxiliary cylinder data are used only for conditional averaging; they are not inserted into the natural exploration filtration. Independent within-cylinder column permutations prove the conditional fixed-count block law.
   Source: lem:ub-reservoir-counts (line 1852), lem:ub-exact-conditional-block-law (line 1985).

5. Conditional first moment.
   Tiny, intermediate and large future cylinders have distinct estimates. Large cylinders use the proved empirical first moment after explicit count perturbations. Good-column refinements, their combinatorial cost, late short tails, current-run contributions and exceptional histories are included. The bound applies at time zero and at every positive-probability natural history satisfying the adapted good event.
   Source: lem:ub-conditional-first-moment (line 2018).

6. Exponential estimate and total variation.
   Stopping adapted unit increments proves the binomial-moment bound without conditioning a history on a future good event. The finite criterion uses 2k directional unit-increment explorations, covers crossing edges, and applies the component truncation on a product of single-matrix events. Its failure probability tends to zero with explicit powers.
   Source: lem:ub-portenko-exponential-moment (line 2237), lem:ub-shared-edge-exponential-moment (line 2307), thm:ub-empirical-upper.

7. Macroscopic and critical-time conclusions.
   Two-sided bounded-scalar quadrature transfers the profile margins to integer empirical endpoints. All parameter choices precede the cut arrays and N. Monotonicity and the existing dominance identities yield the reduced critical-time consequence under the explicit fine-approximation hypothesis. No continuity at the critical time is assumed. The random-environment upper bound uses conditional deterministic laws, a union bound for typical events, and convexity of total variation.
   Source: thm:deterministic-upper-bound, lem:lower-profile-transfer, thm:upper-critical-reduced and their proofs.
